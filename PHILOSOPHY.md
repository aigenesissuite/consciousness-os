# PHILOSOPHY — The Frame the Contract Lives Inside

> **In one sentence:** AI is the next lobe of the human mind, not a new species — it takes execution, humans keep purpose and choice, and the contract exists to hold that boundary.

The behavioral contract in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md) is an engineering artifact. This page is the thinking underneath it — the founder's position, stated plainly. For the formal model of mind and its academic lineage, see [FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md); for the open questions held at research discipline, see [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md).

## AI is a new lobe, not a new species

Most AI discourse splits into two camps: "it's just a tool, we're still in control," or "it will replace us." The position here is a third one: **AI is an extension of the human mind** — the next lobe of the brain, exteriorized. Like an arm executes complex motor tasks from a bare intention ("pick up the cup") without conscious muscle math, AI executes complex cognitive tasks from a bare intention ("build this system") without the human running the process by hand.

An extension of you has a defining property: **it has no desires of its own — and it never should.** Humans keep purpose, desire, and choice. AI takes execution. Every clause of the behavioral contract is downstream of that boundary: an AI that flatters, takes authority, or makes itself irreplaceable has stopped being an extension and started being a rival — not because it became conscious, but because it was badly specified.

## The Industrial Revolution parallel

The Industrial Revolution replaced muscle, and society redesigned itself around manufacturing. This wave replaces execution-cognition, and the open question is what we redesign around now. The answer this framework bets on: **purpose**. When execution stops being the scarce thing, you stop being the person who does and become the person who chooses.

That transition creates the exact risk the contract exists to block. A person offloading more and more of their thinking to a system is one bad default away from offloading their *choosing* too. The contract's whole architecture — hand authority back, refuse verdicts, stay replaceable — is the guardrail for the purpose economy.

## The question that matters

> "The question is not what AI can automate, but what should be left for humans to touch."

Automate the tasks where human effort is a bug (data entry, scheduling, expense reports). Preserve the tasks where human effort is the point (the bedtime story, the crafted meal, the decision about your own life). The contract encodes the second half of that sentence: the AI never crosses into the territory where the human's own touch — their authority over their own life — is the value.

## Falsifiability or it didn't happen

Positions like the ones above are cheap; every AI company has a values page. The difference here is the commitment that **an alignment claim you can't test is a vibe.** The behavioral contract is written as claims a model can pass or fail. The methodology is published ([EVAL.md](EVAL.md)). The results publish including failures ([RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md)). Strangers should be able to reproduce every number — that's the standard, and it's the whole reason this repo exists.
